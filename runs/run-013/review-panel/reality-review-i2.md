## Dimension 2: Goal Achievement -- 6/8

**Assessment:** A CFO searching "contract intelligence" wants to understand (a) what it is, (b) whether it applies to their situation, and (c) what to do about it. The blog delivers on all three. The evaluation framework (new in this iteration) directly addresses the "what to do about it" question, which was the primary gap in iteration 1. However, the blog still focuses more on what contract intelligence does than on the specific financial outcomes CFOs care about (forecast accuracy, budget variance reduction, audit readiness).

**Reader's likely underlying need:** "I know my contract processes are costing us money. I need to quantify the problem and understand what a fix looks like, so I can build a case for change."

**Does content address it?** Partially. The evaluation framework helps quantify the problem ("calculate your exposure"), and the stats provide industry-level cost benchmarks. But the blog does not help the reader build a business case for internal stakeholders. A CFO reading this knows the problem but still cannot present a compelling ROI argument to the board from this content alone.

**Evidence:**
- "Calculate your exposure. What is the total annual value of contracts that renewed in the last year without finance review?" (line 95) -- This directly serves the reader's need to quantify their own problem. Strong.
- "Organizations with contract reporting reduce risk exposure by 30 percent" (line 46) -- Useful but decontextualized. Thirty percent of what? Over what timeframe? A CFO would ask these questions.

## Dimension 6: Feeling Valued -- 2/4

**Assessment:** The content treats the reader as someone with a problem to solve, which is respectful. The evaluation framework is vendor-neutral, which acknowledges the reader's intelligence. However, the blog does not explicitly acknowledge the reader's existing processes or constraints. It does not say "you probably already have a renewal tracking process" or acknowledge budget constraints, team bandwidth, or the politics of platform adoption. The tone is informative-peer but not empathetically-peer.

**Peer treatment test:**
- The content starts from the problem (contracts are invisible) rather than from the reader's current reality (you are probably managing this with spreadsheets and you know it is not working). The difference is subtle but matters for CFOs who want to be met where they are.
- Real-world constraints are not explicitly acknowledged. No mention of budget cycles, IT dependencies, team change management, or competing priorities.
- No "explaining the obvious" moments. The blog does not define "CFO" or explain what a contract is, which is appropriate. But it also does not acknowledge what the reader has already tried.
