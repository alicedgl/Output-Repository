The Cuschieri "two weeks to an afternoon" quote now appears exactly once, in the results section. The misattribution from iteration 1 is fully resolved.

## Dimension 5: Extra Mile Value — 3/4

**Assessment:** This is the dimension with the most improvement, driven by a single sentence that does real work. The 2026 market-positioning statement and the LinkedIn reframe both add perspective that was entirely absent in iteration 1.

**What improved:**

- The 2026 market-signal sentence is the most important addition in the revision: "By 2026, AI-powered contract review is no longer early-adopter territory; it is the standard operating model for high-performing legal teams." This is not just a date stamp. It is a framing device that tells the reader: the question is not whether to adopt AI for contract review, it is whether you can afford to be the legal team that has not. This is a genuine perspective shift. A GC reading this in February 2026 sees their own competitive context reflected back at them. This is the kind of sentence that makes a reader pause and forward the article to their VP.
- The LinkedIn post now includes a specific, useful observation: "Most legal teams adopting AI contract review measure time saved. The bigger win is the risks they finally catch." This is not groundbreaking, but it is a genuine point of view rather than a data-dump-then-engagement-question. It gives the reader something to agree or disagree with, which is what LinkedIn content should do. The pivot from time savings (obvious metric) to risk detection (less obvious metric) is a real insight that a mid-market GC might not have articulated for themselves.
- The closing line ("The next time a portfolio-wide extraction project lands on your desk, it should take an afternoon, not a month. AI contract review makes that possible today.") grounds the CTA in a specific scenario the reader recognizes. It is not a transformative insight, but it is concrete and earned.

**What is still missing:**

- No contrarian or counter-intuitive framing. The content still presents AI contract review as an unambiguous positive with no trade-offs, no caveats, no "here is what most teams get wrong." A genuine extra-mile piece would address the failure modes: when AI extraction goes wrong, what the accuracy looks like on non-standard agreements, why some legal teams have failed at adoption (change management, attorney resistance, poor data quality in legacy contracts). This kind of honest treatment builds more trust than unqualified success stories.
- No framework for evaluation beyond the four questions. A reusable decision framework (e.g., a maturity model for AI readiness, a scoring system for vendor evaluation, a pilot-design checklist) would give the reader a tool they can apply to their specific context. The four questions are useful but static.

**Net assessment:** The 2026 positioning sentence and the LinkedIn reframe are genuine extra-mile additions. The content now offers at least one perspective that makes a reader reconsider an assumption (that AI contract review is still early-adopter territory). The absence of failure-mode transparency and a reusable framework limits the score. +1 from iteration 1.
