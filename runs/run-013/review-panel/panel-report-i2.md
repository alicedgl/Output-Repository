## Content Summary

Three-format content suite for run-013 on contract intelligence for CFOs. Blog (~1,493 words), LinkedIn post (~142 words), and HubSpot email (~220 words body). The blog features eight customer quotes from six companies, an evaluation framework, FAQ section, and four internal links. Topic targets CFOs and VP Finance at mid-market companies ($20M-$200M revenue).

## Panel Consensus

All three critics agree that the five fixes from the modification brief were applied effectively. The evaluation framework (Fix 1) is the highest-impact change, transforming a repetitive takeaways section into actionable self-assessment content. The email time-to-value angle (Fix 5) and the subsection structural variety (Fix 4) also earned unanimous recognition as genuine improvements. The panel agrees that the blog's customer proof layer (eight quotes from six companies) is its primary differentiator and should be preserved in any future revisions.

## Debate Highlights

Two focus areas were debated:

1. **Reader Respect and FAQ repetition:** The Reality Critic and Strategist argued that FAQ stat repetition is format-driven and serves SEO/AEO purposes. The Style Critic countered that transition filler sentences ("The pattern is familiar," "These are not edge cases") independently justify the 6/10 score regardless of FAQ treatment. The Moderator ruled in favor of holding at 6/10 based on the filler evidence.

2. **Data Integrity and unnamed sources:** The Style Critic argued that "legal operations benchmarking data" is the best possible framing given that no named source exists in the source brief. The Strategist held at 3/5, citing six to seven stats without named sources exceeding the 1-2 threshold for a 4/5. The Moderator ruled the Strategist's evidence was definitive and flagged the source gap for future Layer 2 runs.

## Strengths (Consensus)

1. **Evaluation framework** is the blog's strongest section: zero repetition, five independently actionable items, vendor-neutral. All three critics identified this as the highest-value addition.
2. **Customer proof layer** with eight quotes from six distinct companies provides differentiation that no competitor can replicate without their own customer evidence.
3. **Structural variety** in capability subsections (quote-first, question-led, standard, stat-led) eliminates the monotony flagged in iteration 1.
4. **"Flying blind" metaphor thread** creates thematic coherence across title, body, evaluation framework, and closing without feeling forced.

## Areas for Improvement (Non-Blocking)

1. **Reader Respect (Style: 6/10):** Remove transition filler sentences ("The pattern is familiar," "These are not edge cases") that add zero information. This is a surgical edit that would not disrupt the blog's structure.
2. **Feeling Valued (Reality: 2/4):** Add one sentence acknowledging the reader's existing processes or constraints. Something like "If your team is already tracking renewals manually, you know how quickly that system fails at scale" would meet the reader where they are.
3. **Data Integrity (Strategy: 3/5):** This is a structural gap. The nine percent, 71 percent, and $1.5M stats lack named third-party sources. File a Layer 2 data request for future runs to identify or commission specific benchmarking studies that can be cited by name.
4. **Email journey positioning:** The email still leads with awareness-stage stats before transitioning to the consideration-stage implementation angle. A future version could lead with the implementation proof point to more strongly differentiate from the blog.
