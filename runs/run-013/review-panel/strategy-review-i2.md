## Dimension 1: Differentiation -- 4/5

**Assessment:** The blog remains well-differentiated through its customer proof layer. Eight quotes from six distinct companies (Sevita, Denison University, LeoVegas, Pima Community College, Navarino, Meeting Street, PAAY) anchor Concord-specific evidence throughout. The AI-native vs. bolt-on framework is Concord's proprietary positioning, not an industry taxonomy. No competitor could publish this content without stripping the customer quotes and rewriting the AI-native argument.

**Competitor swap test:** A competitor could adapt the problem section (lines 11-22) and the general capability descriptions, but would need to remove all eight customer quotes, the 1,500+ companies/million users proof point, the AI-native positioning framework, and the specific product details (configurable reminders from one to 365 days, extraction on all plans at no additional cost). Approximately 35-40 percent of the blog is Concord-specific.

**Concord-specific elements found:**
- "I just click the button and the AI pulls all the key terms out of the contract." -- Steve Storck, Denison University (line 32)
- "You get natural language querying, automated extraction, and AI-enriched reporting included on every plan at no additional cost" (line 62) -- Concord-specific pricing model claim
- "Concord serves more than 1,500 companies with over one million users" (line 81) -- Verifiable market presence claim
- The entire evaluation framework (lines 83-95) is vendor-neutral by design, but item 4 ("Does your current platform include AI extraction on all plans, or is it a premium add-on?") implicitly differentiates Concord from competitors who charge extra for AI.

**Generic elements found:**
- "Contract intelligence comes down to four capabilities" (line 26) -- Category-level statement
- "Organizations with contract reporting reduce risk exposure by 30 percent" (line 46) -- Industry stat, not Concord-specific
- "Bolt-on AI means the intelligence is an add-on, often locked behind higher-tier plans" (line 64) -- Category critique, not uniquely Concord's to make

## Dimension 3: Cross-Format Coherence -- 4/5

**Assessment:** All three formats share the same core insight (contract data is invisible, causing financial harm) and reference the same anchoring stats (nine percent, 71 percent, $1.5M). The Neville quote appears in all three formats, creating a consistent proof point across touchpoints. Each format provides a different depth: LinkedIn hooks curiosity, the blog educates comprehensively, and the email adds the implementation angle.

**Shared core insight:** Contract data is locked in inaccessible storage, causing measurable financial harm. AI-native contract intelligence makes this data actionable.

**Coherence map:**
- Blog to LinkedIn connection: Both reference the 71 percent and $1.5M stats. Both feature the Neville quote. LinkedIn distills the blog's four capabilities into a single sentence ("the ability to ask questions across your entire agreement portfolio"). Strong synergy.
- Blog to Email connection: Both share the nine percent, 71 percent, and $1.5M stats. Both feature the Neville quote. Email adds implementation time-to-value (new). Blog adds evaluation framework (new). Complementary.
- LinkedIn to Email connection: Both feature Neville. LinkedIn creates curiosity ("When was the last time a contract renewed without your finance team knowing?") that the email addresses with specific proof and a CTA. Logical journey progression.

**Synergy test:** Reading all three gives more than reading each separately. LinkedIn creates curiosity. Blog satisfies it with depth and a self-assessment framework. Email adds the implementation angle and a direct CTA. Yes, synergy is present.
