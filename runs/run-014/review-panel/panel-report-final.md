## Content Summary

Run-014 produced three formats (blog 1,348 words, LinkedIn 176 words, email 254 words) on AI Contract Review for Legal Teams targeting General Counsel / VP Legal personas. The content passed compliance audit in both iterations. Iteration 2 revised all 7 mandatory fixes and 2 of 3 optional improvements from the modification brief. The composite score improved from 65 to 74, clearing the 70-point threshold.

## Panel Consensus

All three critics agree the revision was well-executed: the mandatory fixes were applied surgically without introducing regressions, and the content is materially stronger than iteration 1. The evaluation section transformation (four Concord-specific answers after generic buying criteria) and the 2026 market-positioning sentence were identified as the two highest-value improvements. The critics also agree on the remaining limitations: structural repetition within the blog (the speed claim appears 5 times), the absence of implementation guidance, and the email being the least differentiated format. These are persistent characteristics of the content architecture, not failures of the revision.

## Strengths (Consensus)

1. **Evaluation section is now ownable.** Four Concord-specific sentences (AI in every plan, extraction transparency, 100-doc batch processing, full lifecycle) turn a generic buying guide into competitive intelligence a GC can use for vendor comparison.
2. **2026 market-positioning sentence** does three things: fulfills the title promise, reframes the reader's decision from "should I explore?" to "can I afford not to?", and creates urgency by citing the WorldCC 80% adoption stat.
3. **Quote deployment is strong and clean.** Five distinct customer voices (Sporle, Storck, Cuschieri, Schwartz, Neville) across different organizations and roles. No duplicates. No misattributions. All verified against customer-proof-library.md.
4. **Cross-format coherence** remains the strongest dimension (4/5). Shared proof points create a narrative thread across blog, LinkedIn, and email.

## Areas for Improvement (Non-Blocking)

1. **Deloitte citation** (blog line 14): Add report name and year, or reframe to "research from Deloitte suggests" to avoid a bare appeal to authority. Also add year to the WorldCC 9.2% stat in the opening.
2. **Email subject line**: "Your legal team deserves fewer late nights" is warm but generic. Consider A/B testing with a more specific variant that references the 80-85% time reduction or a trigger event.
3. **LeoVegas framing across formats**: All three formats use the "two weeks to one afternoon" story in nearly identical language. Consider foregrounding Sevita or Denison in the email to create complementary angles.
4. **Structural repetition in blog**: The speed claim appears 5 times across 4 sections. A future editorial pass could merge the "mechanism" and "impact" sections to reduce overlap without losing information.
5. **Implementation guidance**: A companion piece (gated guide or follow-up blog) covering pilot design, change management, and internal stakeholder mapping would fill the largest content gap identified by the Reality Critic.
